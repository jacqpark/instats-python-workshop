Session 3, Roadmap02 / 10
Part 01, from text to features

A document-term matrix.

from sklearn... import CountVectorizer
X = CountVectorizer(
    stop_words='english').fit_transform(text)
# rows are sentences, columns are words

Counting which words appear in which sentence turns text into a table a model can read. TF-IDF downweights common words.

Keep that TF-IDF. It is the baseline your Wednesday fine-tune has to beat.

Session 3, Features03 / 10
Part 02, LDA

Topics are groups of
words that travel together.

LDA reads the matrix and finds those groups. Each topic is a distribution over words. Read the top words and name it.

On the manifesto corpus the topics are recognizable policy areas.

  • Health. health services care mental funding
  • Tax. tax businesses small taxes
  • Defence. defence security industry cyber
  • Crime. rights crime law police
Session 3, LDA04 / 10
Part 02, a caution

Topics are policy areas,
and only some of them lean.

All sentences60% left
Health topic89% left
Defence topic60% right

Read each topic against the 60% baseline. Some lean because one side owns the issue, and others sit near it because both sides write about the same things. LDA finds what parties talk about. Where they stand is a separate question.

That gap is exactly why Wednesday trains a model on the labels directly.

Session 3, Topics and ideology05 / 10
Part 03, meeting STM

The Structural Topic Model
adds a covariate.

LDA ignores who wrote a sentence and when. STM lets a covariate shape topic prevalence. Here the covariate is the election year. Party works the same way.

We run the genuine R stm from Python through rpy2, which leans on the R you already know. A pure-Python cousin is tomotopy's DMR.

# R stm, called from Python
fit = stm(docs, vocab, K=10,
    prevalence=~s(year), data=meta)
estimateEffect(1:10 ~ s(year), fit)
Session 3, STM06 / 10
Part 03, reading the STM result

Read STM through
the fitted curve.

The table

One ingredient per row. Each s(year) row is one piece of a smooth curve. The rows only mean something added together.

The curve

Plot it. plot(eff, "year") shows how each topic's share moves across election years.

Each country gives only its two latest elections, so year partly stands in for country. Why a topic moves is a question for the research design.

Session 3, Hands-on08 / 10
Why this matters

A bag of words
throws away order.

LDA and TF-IDF count words and ignore the order they came in. aid raises trade and trade raises aid look almost the same to them. The next session fixes that.
