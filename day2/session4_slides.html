Why context matters

The same word,
two meanings.

A bag of words gives bank one number. An encoder reads the words around it, so river bank and central bank get different vectors.

That context is what a topic model threw away, and what makes the encoder worth learning.

river bank   → one vector
central bank → a different vector

# context changes the meaning
Session 4, Context02 / 10
What a BERT model is

A bidirectional encoder.

  • Pretrained by hiding words and learning to fill them in.
  • It reads left and right at once, so every word sees its whole sentence.
  • The family. BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa, the multilingual XLM-R, the smaller DistilBERT.
  • A classification head is a small layer on top that turns vectors into a label.
Session 4, The model03 / 10
Tokenization

Text becomes input_ids.

tok('Expand public healthcare.')

input_ids: [101, 7818, 2270, ...]
tokens: ['[CLS]', 'expand',
         'public', ...]

A model reads token ids. The tokenizer splits text into subword tokens and looks up each one. The attention mask marks which tokens are real.

Load the tokenizer that matches your model. They are a pair.

Session 4, Tokenizer04 / 10
Padding, truncation, the wall

Batches are rectangles.

Short sentences are padded, long ones are truncated, so every row is the same length and they stack into a batch.

BERT models cap at 512 tokens. Longer documents get chunked, which is how you scale to whole speeches.

tok(sentences,
    padding=True,
    truncation=True,
    max_length=128)

The model with a head

One forward pass.

Load the encoder with a fresh two-class head and run three sentences through it. The head is untrained, so the numbers are meaningless. The shapes are the point.

Logits become probabilities with softmax, and argmax picks the class.

logits = model(**batch).logits
# shape [3, 2]
probs = logits.softmax(-1)
probs.argmax(-1)
