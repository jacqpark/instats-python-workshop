Day 2 glossary

Topic models, and how a BERT model reads text.
Quasi-sentence
The Manifesto Project's unit of coding. One statement, one code.
cmp_code
The numeric Manifesto category, such as 504 for Welfare State Expansion.
RILE
The standard left-right scale. 13 codes on each side.
Preprocessing
Lowercasing, dropping stopwords, and counting, before any model runs.
Stopwords
Words carrying no topic signal. We also drop country and party names.
Document-term matrix
Rows are documents, columns are words, cells are counts.
CountVectorizer
The sklearn tool that builds that matrix.
TF-IDF
Counts, downweighted for words appearing everywhere. Day 3's baseline.
LDA
Latent Dirichlet Allocation. Finds groups of words that travel together.
Topic
A distribution over words. A policy area, and not a left-right score.
STM
Structural Topic Model. Lets a covariate like year shape topic prevalence.
rpy2
The bridge letting us call the R stm package from inside Python.
Tokenizer
Splits text into the chunks the model reads, and maps them to id numbers.
Token
One of those chunks. Often a whole word, sometimes a word piece.
input_ids
The list of id numbers for your sentence.
attention_mask
1 for a real token, 0 for padding. Tells the model what to ignore.
Padding
Adding filler so every row in a batch is the same length.
Truncation
Cutting text that runs past the limit.
512 tokens
The wall for BERT-family models. Longer documents get chunked.
[CLS] and [SEP]
Markers the tokenizer adds at the start and between segments.
Classification head
The small layer bolted on top that turns numbers into labels.
Logits
The raw scores the head produces, before any normalising.
Softmax
Turns logits into probabilities summing to 1.
Argmax
The position of the largest value, which is the predicted label.
