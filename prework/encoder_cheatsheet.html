What an encoder
model is

One page. No code. Read it once before Day 1.

An encoder reads a piece of text and hands back numbers that stand for its meaning, and that is the whole of what it does. Generative models like ChatGPT continue text instead, which is a different job. This workshop spends its time on the reading kind.

Words become numbers

A computer cannot compare two sentences directly, so the first move in any text-as-data pipeline turns text into numbers. The crude way counts words, which is what a bag of words does, and it throws away order and context. Under that scheme the tax cut helped workers and workers cut the tax help look identical.

An encoder reads the whole sentence at once and gives every word a set of numbers shaped by the words around it. So bank in a sentence about rivers comes out different from bank in a sentence about lending. Those numbers are called embeddings.

Why the name

The family is called BERT, short for Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers. Bidirectional means it reads left to right and right to left together. Encoder means it compresses text into that numeric representation. You will meet BERT, RoBERTa and DeBERTa, all variants of the same idea, plus DistilBERT, a smaller and faster version.

What we do with it

An encoder on its own produces only numbers. To get a label you bolt a small classifier on top, show it examples, and let it learn the mapping from numbers to labels. That is fine-tuning, and it is the Day 3 capstone. A model trained on a few hundred hand-coded sentences can then code hundreds of thousands, which is the reason to bother.

Three words to carry in

Token
A chunk of text the model reads, usually a word or part of one.
Embedding
The list of numbers standing for a token or a sentence.
Fine-tuning
Training a pretrained model further on your own labeled examples.
